Cure is inside the Design District in Shuwaikh, which sets up the wrong expectation. You come in through a retail complex rather than off a street, past a deep maroon frontage with the name above the canopy in both Latin and Arabic — and then the room on the other side is calmer and more considered than the walk in suggests. Terrazzo floor. A burgundy leather banquette running the length of one wall. Pale wood tables, cane-backed chairs, and sheer panels hung between the columns so the space is divided without ever being closed off.

[[image:inline-1|The dining room]]

It's a daylight room first. The glazing is generous and everything in it is light — stone, pale wood, cream fabric — so at lunch it's genuinely bright, and the greenery does more work than the fixtures do. In the evening the uplighting under the banquette takes over and it warms up. They're close to two different rooms, and if you're choosing, lunch is the one that shows the design off.

The seating is mostly twos and fours along that banquette with larger tables through the middle, plus a terrace outside. It's tagged for date nights and for groups, and looking at the room, both hold up — though the banquette side is the better seat, and it's the one to ask for.

[[image:inline-2|A beef plate]]

The kitchen is Japanese at the base with Mediterranean and fusion running alongside it. There's no single signature dish on record, and I'd rather not invent one for you. What there is — and this is the useful part — is a **seasonal specials card**, a short printed list handed over separately from the main menu. On the visit these photos come from it ran to six dishes: a sesame Caesar, charred veggie noodles, orange chicken fries, walnut honey shrimp, a miso ramen, and a coconut mango rice. Ask for it. It changes, and it's the part of the menu that tells you what the kitchen is actually interested in right now.

[[image:inline-3|The terrace]]

On money: it sits in the $$$ band — occasion spending rather than a Tuesday, without being the top of the market. Reservations go through the booking link on the listing, and the full menu is online if you'd rather read it before you commit to the drive out to Shuwaikh.

**One honest thing.** The arrival is the weak part. There's no street-frontage moment, no doorway that announces itself — you're walking through the Design District and the restaurant turns up at the end of it. And it doesn't open until 1:00 PM, so however much that room looks like somewhere you'd want a slow morning, it isn't available for one. Come for a late lunch or come for dinner; there is no third option.
